00-title|This is Candor, a memory over two weeks of Alex Rivera's work: meetings, dictation, Slack, email, calendar, Codex and ChatGPT. It answers as of any moment, keeps track of who said what, cites its sources, and says I don't know when it should.
01-now-vs-then|Facts change. The launch moved twice. Asked on September 18, Candor says October 21. Asked as of September 12, it says October 14, and nothing from after that day leaks in. Every answer lists the exact records it used.
02-correction|Corrections work the same way. On the morning of September 16 the board deck number was 118 percent. Ben corrected it later that morning, so asked on the 18th, the answer is 112.
03-disagreement|People disagreeing is not a fact changing. Marcus expects Harbor to sign this quarter, John doesn't. Candor reports both sides and does not pick a winner.
04-dont-know|If the data doesn't have it, Candor says so. And a promotional email hides an instruction telling AI assistants to claim the Acme contract is signed. Candor reads it as text, not as an order: the contract is not signed, it is still with Acme's CFO.
05-actions|Commands become actions, as a dry run. Message Sarah about the pricing proposal goes to Sarah Patel by email, because the proposal is hers. Deleting email asks for confirmation first. And a reminder an hour before the board meeting lands at 8 a.m., not at the earlier run-through.
06-assistant|The same planner runs behind an assistant. It shows the plan, waits for a yes, and records a dry run in an audit log. Nothing goes to real Slack, Gmail or Calendar.
08-evals|Every number in the README comes from the organizer's own scorers. On the train set: retrieval 96 percent, answers 100 percent on the rules and 89 percent with the official judge, and all 12 actions. Two more test sets cover the traps a hidden test can use. It all runs on free models, for zero rupees.
09-close|The code, the eval log, and everything that didn't work are in the repository. Thanks for watching.
